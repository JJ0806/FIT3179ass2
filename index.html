01

The national picture

Almost one household in ten.

Birds occupy a smaller place in household ownership than dogs or cats, but remain a familiar part of Australian pet keeping.

02

Three maps, three questions

The map changes with the measure.

Ownership rates, approximate household totals and recorded sightings describe different things. The first two maps revisit 2016; the third adds ecological context for three native species familiar to bird keepers.

A sighting is not a pet, or a unique bird. Each hexagon sums human-observation records, including repeated sightings.
03

Behind the household count

There is more than one way to keep birds.

A household count hides the number of birds, the reasons for keeping them and the spaces they live in. National survey categories include backyard poultry as well as companion birds.

04

The everyday commitment

Ownership continues after bringing a bird home.

Spending is one part of the commitment. Services, cleaning and feeding reveal more of the everyday work. Begin with national expenditure, then look closer at reported care practices in Victoria.
